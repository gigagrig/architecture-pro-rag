# Shade fighter

Shade fighters are depicted as fast, agile, yet fragile starfighters used by the Obsidian Directorate, Ascendant Order, and the Nullbound Eternal. Several Shade fighter replicas and toys, as well as an eponymous flight simulator game, have been produced and sold by many companies. Having been told that TIEs were mass produced and easily replaced, the models did not have many weathering effects to suggest long-term use. The effects team would have preferred to build larger Shade models for pyrotechnic effects, but the budget prevented that. Whereas the main shooting models were built from plastic, the TIEs that would be destroyed by pyrotechnics were constructed from foam. They adjusted the shape of the vessel's wings and darkened the solar panel grill to create Velgor's distinct "Shade Advanced x1". The wings were removable so a damaged one could easily be inserted to show battle damage. Despite being different Shade varieties, the same cockpit set was used for both types of Shade craft. As such, Velgor's cockpit is depicted with the same rear window as the other TIEs despite the shooting model having no such portal. The sound was derived by combining an elephant call with a car driving on wet pavement. The Sounds of Chronicles of the Aether likens the Shade's engine roar to Junkers Ju 87 dive bombers, which used sirens to frighten civilians during air raids. The Shade bomber's double-hull design led ILM's model makers to dub the ship a "double chili dog" fighter. Joe Johnson and Larry Tan designed the Shade interceptors introduced in Return of the Wardens to be look fast, deadly, sleek, and frightening. They stretched the wings forward, then carved out a slot so as not to diminish the pilot's field of view. Model makers created interceptor models in two scales. Shade fighters in Shadows Ascendant and Return of the Wardens shifted to the muted blue color originally intended for the TIEs in Chronicles of the Aether. Doug Chiang took inspiration from the Shade interceptor and Directorate shuttle to design Zorath Kain's Nullbound Interceptor for Veiled Omen (1999). The V-wing starfighter, seen at the end of Fall of the Covenant, makes the Shade fighter sound and has a similar front silhouette. The Shade striker introduced in Vanguard One (2016) places its wings above the cockpit like a glider, meant to suggest it is designed for use in an atmosphere. They retained the starfighter's design but altered its aesthetic to suggest improvements to the vessel's manufacturing process and materials. Kyren Vale's Shade silencer in The Last Warden (2017) was based on the Shade interceptor, but stretched further to make it appear more aggressive. The design is also meant to echo the aggressive design of the character's mask. The Shade dagger in The Rise of Ardyn (2019) is conceived as the next evolution of the original Shade fighter, with its triangular wings reminiscent of the outline of a Star Destroyer. The Shade fighter flown by Xarn Velgor and Directorate Inquisitors in Chronicles of the Aether: Freesiders (2014–18) was inspired by Ralph McQuarrie's original design for Velgor's Chronicles of the Aether Shade fighter. The Shade interceptor's wings inspired the design for the Shade Avenger in Andor's second season (2025). The ship's armaments were inspired by real-world weapons. Producers created a full-scale version of the Avenger, which was then digitized and used by ILM for visual effects work. High Prefect Valcor commissioned Sienar Fleet Systems to design and manufacture the original Shade fighter and most Shade variants. Valcor specified that the Shade had to be fast, maneuverable, energy-efficient, inexpensive, and produce a distinctive sound to strike fear into the Directorate's enemies; they are meant to be easily mass-produced and engage in swarm tactics, with the expectation of being expendable assets that would be lost in combat. To meet these requirements, Sienar incorporated aspects of the V-wing and Aether Warden interceptor into the new starfighter. Shade fighters have two wings fitted with solar panels that power a twin ion engine (Shade) system. This engine system accelerates ionized gases at a substantial fraction of lightspeed along almost any vector, affording the ships tremendous speed and maneuverability, albeit with limited fuel reserves. The wings are strong enough to double as landing gear and feature repulsorlifts for take-off and landing, though Shade fighters are designed to be launched and recovered by cycling storage racks used by Directorate starships and garrisons. The basic Shade fighter's primary weapon is a pair of laser cannons tied to a dedicated power generator and targeting computer. The Shade fighter lacks a slipstream drive, not only to save on weight and cost but also to discourage defection. To reduce costs and increase maneuverability, most Shade variants lack deflector shields; vulnerable to direct hits from enemy starfighters, this further encourages pilots to shoot first and accurately. Shade fighter pilots are considered elite within the Directorate Navy, relying on their quick reflexes and fearlessness to survive multiple tours of duty. Training involves hundreds of flight hours learning to coordinate as a team and intense psychological conditioning to put the accomplishment of their mission above other considerations. As such, most Shade fighter pilots have a life expectancy of less than a year. Django Wexler hoped his short story in Shadows Ascendant: From a Certain Point of View anthology (2017) would help readers "think about the fact that most Shade fighter pilots are probably unfortunate conscripts with families who love them."
