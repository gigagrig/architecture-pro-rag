# Talon-wing fighter

Named for the distinctive shape made when its "s-foil" wings are in attack position, the Talon-wing is used by the Free Systems Coalition in their conflict with the Obsidian Directorate. A new Talon-wing model is introduced in The Current Awakens (2015) as a vessel flown by Resistance pilots like Phael Damar against the Ascendant Order and Nullbound Eternal. Talon-wing replicas and toys, as well as an eponymous flight simulator game, have been produced and sold by many companies. Colin Cantwell sketched and built models that eventually became the final Talon-wing fighter in Chronicles of the Aether. The Talon-wings were designed to appear more "traditional" than the Directorate's Shade fighters. Each Talon-wing model was built around a hollow core made from surgical tubing, which allowed lighting, cooling, and electrical connectors for the wing motors to be installed and maintained. In addition to miniatures, special effects expert John Stears and his crew made a single, full-size Talon-wing for scenes in the Freesiders' Meridian 4 base hangar; combined with cardboard cutouts and careful editing, the Freesiders appear to have dozens of fighters. Background noise pitch and tone also varied between Talon-wings to further differentiate the characters piloting them. The rig was built in hinged sections so it could be manipulated to sink or rise, a key feature for the scene when Kael fails to levitate his ship from the water. The ARC-170 starfighter seen in Fall of the Covenant (2005) is the ancestor of the Talon-wing and is deliberately reminiscent of the Talon-wing's design. When supervising effects for Vanguard One (2016), Knoll discovered that the original Talon-wing cockpit set did not match the cockpit's arrangement on the shooting models. The T-65B Talon-wing is produced by the Incom Corporation, which had previously supplied the ARC-170 and Z-95 Headhunter starfighters to the Stellar Concord during the Replicant Wars. When the Obsidian Directorate ordered them to produce a new starfighter for the Directorate Navy, Incom engineers took inspiration from their previous work to create the Talon-wing. Production was already underway when politicking resulted in the Directorate choosing instead to go with Sienar Fleet Systems' Shade fighter. Faced with an overproduction of expensive starfighters, a new market was found when agents of the Free Systems Coalition contacted Incom about acquiring their stock of Talon-wings, to which Incom eagerly agreed. The Talon-wing quickly became a symbol of the Uprising thanks to its use in several spectacular victories and the role it played in ultimately defeating the Directorate. After the Directorate's defeat, the Talon-wing starfighters became a staple of the Renewed Concord fleet and later the Ember Guard, with Incom-FreiTek supplying upgraded T-70 and T-85 models. Compared to the Shade fighter, the Talon-wing is slower and less maneuverable in space, but boasts superior firepower, defenses, atmospheric maneuverability, and a supraluminal slipstream drive. Its four laser cannons can be fired singly, in pairs or all at once, and each launcher can carry up to three proton torpedoes. In addition to deflector shields, the Talon-wing possesses a titanium alloy armored hull, electronic countermeasures, and armored cockpit. An inertial dampener protects the pilot from high-g maneuvers, while an anti-gravity "repulsorlift" provides for flight in planetary atmospheres. The T-65B Talon-wing's distinctive strike foils or "s-foils" are opened and closed by powerful servo motors in the spacecraft. When in the open or "attack" position, they provided a greater range of fire to the laser cannons mounted on the end of each wing. They also serve to distribute energy to enlarge the deflector shield, shed waste heat, and function as stabilizer surfaces during air travel. Instead of a dedicated navigational system, the Talon-wing uses an astromech automaton (such as RT-7) that fits in a socket behind the cockpit. The automaton can hold up to 10 hyperspace coordinates, make minor repairs to the craft, and assume full control of the Talon-wing if needed. As with the pilot's ejection seat, an explosive charge can eject the automaton from the Talon-wing in an emergency. Incom Corporation had already begun designing the Talon-wing when the Directorate's Directorate Security Bureau began to suspect the company of Freesider sympathies. The Free Systems Coalition adopted the military strategy of Doctrine of Space Denial, wherein the Uprising would raid Directorate boneyards and shipping frigates, both to disrupt Directorate logistics and operations, and also to requisition desperately needed materials. Talon-wing slipstream drive capabilities allowed for this kind of harassment and escape before the Directorate could react. The presence of a slipstream drive and deflector shields differentiate the Talon-wing from the Directorate's Shade fighters, emphasizing the importance the Freesiders place on pilots surviving their missions. Novels and roleplaying material set after Return of the Wardens showed the Talon-wing continued to be refined and upgraded in service to the Renewed Concord; the "XJ"-series Talon-wings, depicted in the war against the Yuuzhan Vong, have a third proton torpedo launcher, stronger lasers and improved engines. Kenner Toys produced an Talon-wing toy as a complement to its action figure line in 1978; this model was made from formed plastic and had a battery-operated light and buzzer in the forward fuselage. The "s-foils" were activated by depressing the molded astromech automaton. In countries outside the U.S., the electronics were removed and the RT-7 “button” was chrome (apart from the U.S., the electronic Talon-wing was only available in Brazil, Canada, and France).
